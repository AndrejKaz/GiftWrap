A good fit is a **relational PostgreSQL schema**. This is a design suggestion only—I haven’t added database files or changed the project.

```text
users ──< sessions
  ├────< carts ──< cart_items ──< custom_box_items
  ├────< wishlist_items >── products
  └────< orders ──< order_items ──< order_item_contents

products ──< product_categories >── categories
products ──< product_images
products ──< product_pet_types >── pet_types
box_sizes ──< custom_box_items
box_sizes ──< gift_sets ──< gift_set_items >── products
```

### Main tables

- **users** — account ID, unique username, `password_hash`, creation date. Never store plain-text passwords.
- **sessions** — session ID, user ID, expiry. Supports persistent sign-in.
- **categories** — category name and optional `parent_id`, so categories can be nested, e.g. `Pets → Dogs → Toys`.
- **products** — name, description, brand, SKU, price in cents, active status, and flags such as `age_restricted`.
- **product_categories** — links products to one or more categories.
- **product_images** — product ID, image URL, alt text, display order.
- **pet_types** and **product_pet_types** — identify which pets a product is for, such as dogs, cats, birds, or fish.
- **box_sizes** — box name, dimensions, capacity, and price.
- **gift_sets** and **gift_set_items** — represent premade gift boxes and the products included in each.
- **carts** and **cart_items** — a user’s current bag. A line item is either a regular product or a selected box size; custom box contents go in `custom_box_items`.
- **wishlists** and **wishlist_items** — saved products for each user.
- **orders**, **order_items**, and **order_item_contents** — order records and snapshots of product names/prices and custom-box contents at purchase time. Snapshots preserve order history if the catalog changes.
- **addresses** — optional saved delivery addresses. Copy the chosen address into the order when placing it, so later address edits don’t alter old orders.
- **newsletter_subscriptions** — optional email subscriptions, independent of user accounts.

### Important constraints

- Store prices as integer cents, such as `2599` for `$25.99`.
- Require a cart item to be either a product or a custom box—not both.
- Enforce each box’s capacity when adding its contents.
- Use foreign keys and unique constraints to prevent duplicate usernames, duplicate wishlist entries, and invalid references.
- Store payment-provider IDs and payment status only; **do not store card numbers or security codes**.
- For age-restricted products, record and enforce the required checks during checkout.

The key modeling choice is to keep **recipient types, product categories, and individual pets as separate concepts**. That makes it possible to show a product in multiple relevant categories without duplicating the product record.
